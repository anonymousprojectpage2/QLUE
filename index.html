Abstract

Human teleoperation is a widely used approach for collecting real-world demonstrations for robot policy learning, enabling robots to learn manipulation skills directly from human supervision. However, collecting such demonstrations is costly, and their execution quality can vary substantially. Even trajectories ranked lower by data curation may still contain useful supervision, such as corrective actions following failed attempts. Preserving this supervision is particularly important when the available demonstrations are limited. We propose QLUE (Quality-Aware Learning via Usefulness Estimation of Demonstrations), a framework that estimates demonstration usefulness and adjusts training contributions while retaining the collected dataset. QLUE uses normalized imitation-loss gradients at the pretrained policy initialization to estimate usefulness without requiring separate validation demonstrations or task-specific reward models. Learning Coherence measures agreement with the consensus of other demonstrations, while Incremental Learning Signal captures differences in parameter-wise update emphasis. The resulting trajectory scores guide behavior cloning (BC) through an interpolation between uniform and quality-based weighting. We evaluate QLUE through real-world manipulation experiments and simulation studies using demonstration datasets with controlled stop, detour, and recovery behaviors. Across three real-world tasks, QLUE increases the average success rate of π0 from 62.1% with uniform BC to 77.1%, a gain of 15.0 percentage points. Complementary experiments in ManiSkill3 compare the proposed weighting strategy with quality-based subset selection and assess the contributions of the two quality measures.

Method

Quality estimation uses only the collected demonstrations and the pretrained policy, before any downstream training.

  • Learning Coherence measures agreement between a trajectory's update and the consensus update of the other demonstrations.
  • Incremental Learning Signal captures differences in parameter-wise update emphasis relative to the consensus.
  • Demonstration Quality Score combines both and guides behavior cloning without discarding any demonstration.
